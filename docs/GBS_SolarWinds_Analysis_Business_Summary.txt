GBS SERVICE HEALTH DASHBOARD
SolarWinds LAN Health Pillar
Analysis & Design Summary --- Business Review
============================================
Prepared by: BMI Technology | Infrastructure Architecture Team
April 2026 | For Business Approval


BACKGROUND
----------
HPE Aruba Central API access is not currently available. The client
has confirmed that SolarWinds Observability Self-Hosted (version
2025.1.1) is deployed and monitoring the organisation's LAN network.
This document summarises the analysis and proposed design for adding
SolarWinds LAN data as a new pillar in the GBS Service Health Dashboard.


WHAT SOLARWINDS ADDS TO THE DASHBOARD
--------------------------------------
The GBS dashboard currently shows wireless health (from a data file
pending Aruba API access). SolarWinds provides visibility into the
wired LAN infrastructure underneath the wireless layer.

When a GBS user cannot connect, the issue could be at either layer --
wireless or wired. With both pillars visible in one screen, the
support team can identify the root cause immediately without logging
into multiple systems.

SolarWinds is NOT a replacement for the wireless pillar. It is an
additional, complementary view.


WHAT WAS CONFIRMED DURING ANALYSIS
------------------------------------
The SolarWinds environment was queried directly during analysis:

  - 700+ LAN devices (nodes) are monitored
  - Devices span 30 country locations
  - 100 active alerts are present at any given time
  - 11 devices are currently offline
  - Each device has one monitored network interface
  - Devices have a Country property that can be used for filtering
  - 50 devices have no country assigned (data quality gap)

These numbers confirm SolarWinds has sufficient data to produce
a meaningful LAN health view for the dashboard.


THE CHALLENGE -- TOO MANY DEVICES TO DISPLAY
---------------------------------------------
700 devices across 30 countries cannot be shown as individual rows
on a dashboard. The design solves this in two levels:

  Level 1 -- Global View (default):
  The dashboard shows one summary row per country -- 30 rows.
  Each row shows that country's device count, availability
  percentage, packet loss, and active alerts. The overall LAN
  health score is computed across all 700 devices.

  Level 2 -- Country View (on selection):
  A Country selector in the LAN pillar header allows the user
  to filter down to a specific country or region group, showing
  individual devices for that selection only.


COUNTRY SELECTOR DESIGN
------------------------
The selector offers three levels of choice:

  All            -- global view across all 30 countries
  Region groups  -- e.g. Asia Pacific, EMEA, Americas
                    (configured by the client team)
  Individual     -- any single country, with device count shown

Region groups are fully configurable by the client directly in the
database -- no technical change required to add, rename, or
reorganise groups. Example groups proposed:

  Asia Pacific : covers relevant Asia Pacific countries
  EMEA         : covers relevant Europe, Middle East, Africa countries
  Americas     : covers relevant Americas countries

The 50 unassigned devices are shown as an "Unclassified" group --
making the gap visible rather than hiding it, and prompting the
SolarWinds team to correct the missing data.


HEALTH SCORING
--------------
Each device receives a LAN Health Score (0-100) based on three factors:

  Device availability    -- is the device up, degraded, or down?
  Packet loss            -- is data reaching the device reliably?
  Interface utilisation  -- is the network link under stress?
  Alert penalty          -- deducted for active critical or warning alerts

Country and overall scores are weighted by device count -- a country
with 250 devices has proportionally more influence on the global
score than a country with 10 devices.


WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
---------------------
The existing dashboard pillars -- Wireless, AVD, Applications, and
Printing -- are completely unchanged. The SolarWinds pillar is added
alongside them, not in place of any existing pillar.
